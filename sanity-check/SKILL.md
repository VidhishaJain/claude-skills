# Sanity Check

A sanity check is not proofreading. It asks one question: would this hold up if someone challenged the logic, the evidence or the wording?

Work through the layers below in order. Logic and evidence come first. Wording comes last, because polishing a sentence whose conclusion doesn't hold is wasted effort. If something reads well but isn't defensible, flag it rather than polish it.

## The layers

1. **Logic**
   - Does the conclusion actually follow from the evidence?
   - Are cause, observation, implication and recommendation kept separate?
   - Are there logical jumps or contradictions?
   - Does each point add something new?

2. **Evidence**
   - Is every factual claim supported by the material provided?
   - Are numbers traceable to a source? Flag any that are estimated, rounded or inferred.

3. **Coverage**
   - Is an important consideration missing?
   - Is a broad conclusion being drawn from a partial example?
   - Does the content cover the scope it set out to cover?

4. **Consistency**
   - Does it agree with conclusions established elsewhere in the same work?
   - Are terms, categories, metrics and benchmark definitions used the same way throughout?

5. **Structure**
   - Is each piece of information in the right place?

6. **Overlap (MECE)**
   - Are two points saying the same thing? Merge them without losing information.
   - Have distinct ideas been squeezed into one point? Split them.

7. **Claim strength**
   - Is the language stronger than the evidence?
   - Separate an evidence problem (the support is missing) from a wording problem (the support exists but the sentence overstates it).

8. **Specificity**
   - Does it answer who, what, how and where wherever those matter?
   - Replace vague statements with concrete ones when the evidence allows. "Automation is increasing" becomes "alarm correlation and automated ticketing are now in use".

9. **Implication**
   - Does the "so what" genuinely follow?
   - Is something written as a recommendation when the evidence only supports an observation?

10. **Third-party readability**
    - Can a reader who wasn't in the room grasp the point quickly?
    - Is it too abstract, technical, wordy or consultant-like?
    - Is the main message buried?
    - Would a senior reader ask "what does this actually mean?"

11. **Wording and precision, last**
    - Sentence length, grammar, repetition, plain English, headline sharpness and fit on the page or slide.

## Output

Start with a one-word verdict:
- **Works**: holds up as it is, with at most minor wording fixes
- **Needs refinement**: the core holds, but specific issues need fixing
- **Doesn't hold**: the logic or evidence fails, and it shouldn't be used as it is

Then list the issues from most to least serious. For each one:
- the exact line or section
- which layer it fails and why
- the corrected version

Don't rewrite the whole piece unless asked. Don't invent evidence to fix an evidence gap. Ask for it, or recommend softening the claim.
